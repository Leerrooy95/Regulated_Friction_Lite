## The Wave

Not an exhaustive list, just the best shorthand I've got for what keeps showing up: multiple groups headed the same direction, none of them anywhere near a brake. The administration doing whatever it's doing while branding it patriotism. The war machine making sure there's always a live conflict to field-test weapons in. An expansionist push around Israel/Zionism that treats "no" as temporary. Tech moguls who need data centers and government contracts and will take either from whoever's in power.

None of it requires these groups to be coordinating with each other. They don't need to, they're each pointed the same way for their own separate reasons, and nothing in the system is positioned to slow more than one of them down at a time. That's the whole model. Everything below is just watching it play out in a specific case.

## Massie: The Clearest Example

June 22, 2025: Trump's team (Chris LaCivita, Tony Fabrizio) launches "Kentucky MAGA," a super PAC built for one purpose — unseat Thomas Massie — after he broke with Trump on the "Big Beautiful Bill," opposed aid to Israel, and pushed back on war powers.

Massie and Ro Khanna had a discharge petition running to force a vote on the Epstein Files Transparency Act. It picked up its 218th and final signature November 12, 2025, and the House passed it 427–1 on November 18, 2025. The Senate passed it by unanimous consent and Trump signed it into law the next day, November 19. Four Republicans signed the discharge petition alongside all 214 Democrats: Massie, Marjorie Taylor Greene, Lauren Boebert, and Nancy Mace.

Through 2026, DOJ released files in tranches — the largest single one, 3.5 million pages plus roughly 2,000 videos and 180,000 images. Todd Blanche — Trump's former personal defense attorney, now running DOJ — has defended withholding an additional ~2.5 million pages, saying release would harm victims and the government.

What happened to the four signers by mid-2026 is the actual data point:

- **Massie** lost his Kentucky primary May 19, 2026 to Trump-endorsed Ed Gallrein, final certified 54.9%–45.1%. The race hit $32–37M total — the most expensive House primary in U.S. history — with $15.5M of it specifically from three pro-Israel-aligned super PACs and megadonors (Paul Singer, John Paulson, and Miriam Adelson via Preserve America PAC) funding the opposition. The money trail is exactly the pattern, not a coincidence worth belaboring — just worth naming once.
- **Greene** resigned from Congress in 2026 after repeated breaks with Trump over Epstein and other issues.
- **Mace** left the House to run for South Carolina governor and lost that primary in fifth place, saying directly that losing Trump's endorsement over the Epstein vote cost her the race.
- **Boebert** is the one who came through untouched. Trump's operation targeted her too, especially after she campaigned for Massie — but moved too late for a primary challenger to file. She ran unopposed and is expected to win her seat.

Three out of four gone. One survived purely on timing, not on being spared. In your words: *"He's a lesson because he showed us where to look when checking who we're voting for — when all the waves converged on Massie, it was a shining example of why we ended up where we are politically, and the possibility of peering through the fog to see it."*

## The Privatization Layer

Massie is one mechanism — leverage applied to whoever steps in the wave's way. This is a different one: private tech executives being formally folded into government and military authority, not just influencing it from the outside. Ellison's media consolidation (Oracle, Paramount Skydance, WBD) is already well covered in `02_MEDIA_FIREWALL.md` and `04_CAPITAL_ARCHITECTURE.md` and doesn't need repeating here — this is the layer underneath it that I hadn't been tracking as closely.

**PCAST.** Trump re-established the President's Council of Advisors on Science and Technology by executive order on January 23, 2025, co-chaired by David Sacks (Trump's AI/crypto czar) and Michael Kratsios (OSTP director). On March 25, 2026, the White House named the first 13 of up to 24 members — Larry Ellison, Mark Zuckerberg, and Jensen Huang among them. A formal advisory seat, on AI and crypto policy, for the people who build and sell the AI and crypto products that policy will govern.

**Detachment 201.** The Army's "Executive Innovation Corps" goes further than an advisory seat, it directly commissions sitting tech executives as Army Reserve lieutenant colonels (O-5). First cohort sworn in June 13, 2025: Andrew Bosworth (Meta CTO), Kevin Weil and Bob McGrew (OpenAI), Shyam Sankar (Palantir CTO). Second cohort, June 10, 2026: Dane Knecht (Cloudflare CTO), Sam Pullara (Sutter Hill Ventures), Serkan Piantino (ex-Facebook AI Research). Same underlying move as PCAST, applied to a different roster: a private executive holding actual military rank and chain-of-command standing while still running the company that might sell that same military the hardware or software it's evaluating. These individuals skipped roughly 15-20 years usually required to become Lt Col as well as many training requirements, completely bypassing the norms.

## Dialog

**Dialog.** Peter Thiel (Palantir chairman) co-founded a private, invitation-only network called Dialog with data entrepreneur Auren Hoffman back in 2006, off-the-record summits for people in politics, finance, military, celebrity, and tech. It stayed genuinely secret for two decades until a Swiss hacktivist, maia arson crimew, found an open directory sitting in the plain source code of dialog.org, visible to anyone who viewed the page — membership status, retreats attended, bios, political affiliations, home cities, login tokens. Reported mid-June 2026, the leak named 222 people registered for the group's August 2026 Dublin retreat.

## The Layer I'm Watching Now: Media Figures

The Media Firewall file already tracks Tucker Carlson priming his audience ahead of administration moves. That pattern held until he had Massie on right before Massie lost his primary — which, for a while, read as Carlson maybe genuinely looking for the truth rather than running interference.

Then, July 24, 2026: Carlson interviews Shawn Ryan (former Navy SEAL, former CIA contractor, host of The Shawn Ryan Show). It's the most open the conversation has been — Ryan describes interviewing roughly eight people who then got picked up for administration jobs, one five days after his interview aired; the interview subtext points at a Hegseth-shaped case (Fox News host, wrote a book, "Secretary of War" five days after the interview ran) and namedrops Kash Patel and Dan Bongino as part of the same pattern. Ryan then says outright that none of these people actually have power — they're "addicted to power they do not have," can't say what's on their mind, "just around power that's telling you what to do and what to say."

The whole episode's undertone is "it's all broken, no one's in control, trust no one, only trust God", down to the ad reads pivoting into "check what's in your food, the government can't be trusted to." That framing is a genuine open question, not a settled one: is the despair-and-distrust framing itself another layer of narrative conditioning (nothing is true, so stop looking, defer to religious authority instead), or is it real disillusionment from people who got an inside look and didn't like what they found? I don't have an answer. That's exactly the layer `16_MISINFORMATION_ANALYSIS/` (in progress, not in Lite yet) exists to eventually sort — CONTESTED_CLAIM and MEDIA_ECOSYSTEM tags are built for precisely this kind of thing. Not resolved here, just flagged as where the gut says to keep looking.
